## Section 1: Algorithmic Bias — When AI Discriminates

### What is Algorithmic Bias?

Algorithmic bias occurs when an AI system produces unfair or discriminatory results. This happens because:

1. **Training Data Bias**: If the data is biased, the AI learns bias
2. **Selection Bias**: Who is included/excluded from the data
3. **Measurement Bias**: How data is collected and labelled
4. **Aggregation Bias**: Treating all groups the same when they differ

### Caribbean-Specific Examples

**Facial Recognition in the Caribbean:**
- Most facial recognition AI is trained on European/Asian faces
- Performance drops significantly for darker-skinned Caribbean people
- A study showed error rates of 35% for dark-skinned women vs 1% for light-skinned men
- Impact: False arrests, denied access to services, surveillance abuse
- This affects Jamaica, Trinidad, Barbados, Haiti — ALL of us

**Credit Scoring in Caribbean Banks:**
- AI credit models trained on US/European financial patterns
- Caribbean economic realities are different: informal economy, remittance-based income, seasonal tourism work
- A Jamaican higgler (market vendor) with steady income could be denied credit because the AI doesn't understand informal commerce
- Guyanese rice farmers with irregular but reliable income flagged as "high risk"

**Healthcare AI:**
- Skin cancer detection AI trained mostly on lighter skin — dangerous for Caribbean populations
- Drug dosage algorithms that use race as a factor — problematic given our mixed-heritage population
- Medical AI that doesn't account for sickle cell trait prevalence in Caribbean populations

**Language Bias:**
- Voice assistants struggle with Caribbean accents and Creole languages
- Patois, Trinidadian Creole, Haitian Kreyol, Papiamento — all poorly served
- AI translation tools inadequate for Caribbean languages
- This creates a digital divide within our own region

## Section 2: Data Sovereignty — Who Owns Caribbean Data?

### The Problem

Caribbean nations generate valuable data — but who controls it?

- **Tourism Data**: Visitor patterns, preferences, spending — often held by foreign hotel chains and airlines
- **Health Data**: Caribbean patient records processed by US/European cloud services
- **Agricultural Data**: Crop yields, soil data collected by international organisations
- **Marine Data**: Fishing patterns, ocean currents, reef health — who owns this knowledge?
- **Financial Data**: Banking data processed through international payment systems

### Why It Matters

- **Digital Colonialism**: Foreign companies extract Caribbean data like colonial powers extracted sugar and bauxite
- **Loss of Control**: If yuh data is on a server in Virginia, USA, Caribbean privacy laws may not apply
- **Economic Leakage**: Caribbean data creates value — but the profits flow overseas
- **National Security**: Government data on foreign servers creates vulnerability

### What Caribbean Nations Can Do

1. **Data Localisation Laws**: Require certain data to stay in the Caribbean (Barbados and Jamaica exploring this)
2. **Regional Data Centres**: CARICOM-operated cloud infrastructure
3. **Data Cooperatives**: Caribbean communities collectively own and govern their data
4. **Open Data Standards**: Share data openly within the region while controlling external access
5. **Indigenous Data Sovereignty**: Respect First People communities' (Garifuna, Kalinago, Taino descendants) rights to their cultural data

## Section 3: AI and Employment in the Caribbean

### Tourism Sector — The Big Vulnerability

Tourism employs 2+ million people across the Caribbean. AI threatens:

- **Hotel front desk** → AI check-in kiosks (already in Cancun resorts)
- **Tour guides** → AR/VR guided experiences with AI narration
- **Travel agents** → AI booking systems (already dominant)
- **Restaurant staff** → Automated ordering and service robots
- **Transportation** → Self-driving tour buses and taxis

**But Caribbean tourism has strengths AI cannot replace:**
- Genuine Caribbean warmth and hospitality ("liming" culture)
- Cultural authenticity — real steelpan, real jerk chicken, real stories
- Personal connections and community-based tourism
- Spontaneous, organic experiences that AI cannot replicate

### Agriculture Sector

- **Threat**: Automated farming could displace workers in Guyana, Belize, Jamaica
- **Opportunity**: AI can help small farmers compete — precision agriculture, market prediction, pest detection
- **Caribbean Advantage**: Small-scale farming and unique crops (ackee, breadfruit, dasheen) not easily automated

### Financial Services

- **Threat**: AI replacing bank tellers, loan officers, accountants in Cayman, BVI, Barbados
- **Opportunity**: AI-powered financial inclusion for the unbanked population (significant in Jamaica, Haiti, Guyana)
- **Strategy**: Upskill Caribbean workers to manage and develop AI systems, not just be replaced by them

### The Way Forward

1. **Education**: This AI curriculum! Train the next generation (that's YOU!)
2. **Regulation**: Caribbean governments must create AI employment frameworks
3. **Entrepreneurship**: Build Caribbean AI companies, don't just consume foreign AI
4. **Hybrid Approach**: AI augments Caribbean workers, not replaces them
5. **New Jobs**: AI trainer, data curator, ethics auditor, AI-tourism designer — new Caribbean careers

## Section 4: Cultural Preservation Through AI

### AI as a Tool for Caribbean Heritage

While AI poses risks, it also offers powerful tools for cultural preservation:

**Language Preservation:**
- AI can help document and preserve Caribbean Creole languages before they fade
- Speech recognition for Patois, Kreyol, Papiamento, Garifuna
- AI-powered dictionaries and learning tools for indigenous languages
- Automated transcription of oral histories from Caribbean elders

**Music and Art:**
- AI analysis of Caribbean music patterns: calypso, reggae, dancehall, soca, zouk, kompa
- Digital preservation of traditional art forms
- AI can identify and catalogue Caribbean artistic heritage
- But watch out: AI-generated "Caribbean music" without Caribbean artists is cultural appropriation

**Oral History and Storytelling:**
- Caribbean culture is rich in oral tradition — Anancy stories, calypso commentary, spoken word
- AI transcription and translation can preserve these treasures
- Natural Language Processing adapted for Caribbean speech patterns
- Digital archives of Caribbean stories accessible to future generations

**Historical Records:**
- AI can help digitise and analyse colonial-era documents
- Reveal hidden histories of enslaved peoples across the Caribbean
- Connect diaspora communities with their Caribbean heritage
- Restore damaged historical documents and photographs

### The Cultural Appropriation Warning

- AI trained on Caribbean culture could be used to create "Caribbean" content without Caribbean people
- Reggae beats generated by AI in Silicon Valley? Calypso lyrics written by machines?
- We must ensure Caribbean people control how their culture is used in AI
- **Principle**: Nothing about us, without us.

## Section 5: Environmental AI Ethics in the Caribbean

### Climate Justice and AI

The Caribbean contributes less than 1% of global carbon emissions but faces the worst effects of climate change. AI in this context raises ethical questions:

- **Who benefits from climate AI?** If prediction models serve insurance companies who then raise Caribbean premiums, that's not justice
- **Data gaps**: Climate models often lack granular Caribbean data — AI predictions may be unreliable for our region
- **Green AI**: Training large AI models consumes massive energy — should Caribbean nations invest in AI training or renewable energy?
- **Just transition**: As Caribbean economies move from fossil fuels (Trinidad) to green energy, AI should support workers, not just optimise profits

### Marine Conservation Ethics

- **Surveillance vs Privacy**: AI-powered cameras monitoring fishing boats — conservation or control?
- **Traditional Knowledge**: Caribbean fishers have generations of ocean knowledge — AI should complement, not override this
- **Equitable Access**: Should AI-based fishing route optimisation be available to small fishers or only big companies?

## Section 6: Building Ethical AI for the Caribbean

### Caribbean AI Ethics Framework (Proposed)

1. **Ubuntu/Community First**: AI should benefit the community, not just individuals or corporations. "I am because we are."
2. **Transparency**: Caribbean people should understand how AI decisions affecting them are made. No black boxes for consequential decisions.
3. **Inclusivity**: AI must work for ALL Caribbean people — every skin tone, accent, language, island, economic status.
4. **Data Sovereignty**: Caribbean data should be governed by Caribbean people under Caribbean laws.
5. **Cultural Respect**: AI should preserve and celebrate Caribbean culture, never exploit or appropriate it.
6. **Environmental Responsibility**: AI development must consider Caribbean environmental vulnerability.
7. **Human Dignity**: AI must never diminish the dignity of Caribbean people. Humans make final decisions on matters of justice.
8. **Accountability**: Clear responsibility chains for AI harms. Who answers when AI goes wrong?
9. **Benefit Sharing**: If Caribbean data creates AI value, Caribbean communities should share in the benefits.
10. **Youth Empowerment**: Invest in Caribbean youth (like YOU!) as AI creators, not just AI consumers.

### What You Can Do Right Now

- **Question AI tools you use**: How was this trained? Whose data? Who benefits?
- **Report bias**: When yuh see AI behaving unfairly, speak up!
- **Learn to build**: Don't just use AI — learn to create it (you're doing this!)
- **Represent Caribbean**: Bring yuh Caribbean perspective to global AI conversations
- **Support local**: Use and promote AI tools built by Caribbean developers
- **Stay informed**: Follow AI ethics research from UWI, Caribbean tech communities
